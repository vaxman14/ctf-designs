There's a sentence I hear from small business owners all the time: "Nobody's going to hack us. We're too small."

I get why it feels true. You picture a hacker as a person, sitting somewhere, choosing a victim. And why would that person choose a five-person plumbing company in Murrieta over a bank?

Here's the problem. That person doesn't exist. Not for you, anyway.

## Nobody is choosing you. That's the point.

The overwhelming majority of attacks on small business websites aren't done by people at all. They're done by scripts. Automated programs that crawl the internet around the clock, testing every site they find against a list of known weaknesses. Old plugin? Exposed config file? Login page with no protection? The script doesn't know your name and doesn't care about your revenue. It only knows one thing: the door opened.

So the real question was never "why would anyone target me?" It's "am I easy?"

A big company has a security team, monitoring, and lawyers. A small business has a website somebody built in 2021 and hasn't touched since. If you were writing a script to break into things at scale, which one would you point it at?

## What "easy" actually looks like

Easy isn't dramatic. Easy is boring. Easy is:

- Software that hasn't been updated in a year, with holes that were publicly documented and patched long ago
- An admin login sitting at the same address as everyone else's, with a password someone picked in a hurry, or reused between 25 different projects
- Files that were never supposed to be public, sitting one guessable URL away
- No security headers, so browsers do nothing to protect your visitors

None of that requires a genius to exploit. That's exactly why it gets exploited. The scripts win on volume. They knock on a million doors, and the unlocked ones lose.

## The part that stings

When a small business gets hit, the damage isn't abstract. Your site starts serving spam or malware, Google flags it, and your search ranking that took years to build evaporates. Customer info leaks and now you have legal exposure. In California that can mean real money per record. Or your site just goes down during the busiest week you've had all year.

And the worst part is, most of these hits were preventable with an hour of maintenance. Not enterprise security. Basics.
