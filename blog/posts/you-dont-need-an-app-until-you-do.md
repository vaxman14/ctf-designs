I talk a lot of people out of building apps.

That surprises folks, since building apps is part of what I do at CTF Designs. But most small businesses that think they need an app actually need a better website, and most processes that feel painful just need a decent spreadsheet. An app built for the wrong reason is an expensive way to feel modern.

And then there's the other group. The ones who genuinely crossed the line and don't realize it, so they keep duct-taping a process that's actively costing them money. This post is about knowing which group you're in.

## The spreadsheet has become a person

Here's the clearest sign. Somewhere in your business there's a spreadsheet that one specific person understands. It has tabs nobody else opens, color codes that live in that person's head, and formulas everyone is afraid to touch. When that person is on vacation, the process stops.

That's not a spreadsheet anymore. That's unwritten software with a single point of failure. When knowledge about how your business runs lives in one head, you don't have a tool problem, you have a risk problem, and an app is how you write that knowledge down in a form that works without them.

## You're retyping the same information twice

Watch your own workflow for a day and count how many times a piece of information gets typed into a second place. Customer details from email into an invoice. Job info from a text message into a schedule. Numbers from one system into another every Friday.

Every retype is time, and every retype is a chance for an error that a customer eventually sees. If information regularly makes the same trip between two places, that trip should be automatic. This is the single most common thing I build: not a flashy product, just a bridge that removes retyping.

## Your process breaks when volume goes up

Some processes work fine at ten customers and quietly fall apart at forty. Follow-ups get missed. Jobs get double-booked. Invoices go out late, which means money comes in late. If your growth plan makes you nervous because you know the current system won't survive it, that nervousness is data. The time to build the sturdier version is before the wave, not during it.

## You're saying "no" to money because of logistics

This one hides in plain sight. A booking you turned down because scheduling is chaos. A service you don't offer because tracking it would be a nightmare. An area you won't cover because coordinating it manually is too much. When operations limit revenue, an app isn't a cost anymore. It's a locked door with income behind it.

## What's changed: the math

The reason this decision is worth revisiting in 2026 is that the cost side moved. Custom software used to mean months and a terrifying invoice, which meant only the biggest problems justified it. AI-assisted development changed that. Small, focused tools that solve exactly one business problem can now be built in days. The bar for "worth it" dropped dramatically, and a lot of business owners are still pricing it in their heads at 2019 rates.

## The honest test

If none of the four signs above hit home, keep the spreadsheet. Sincerely. It's free and it works.
